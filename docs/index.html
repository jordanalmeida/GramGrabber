Built to empty a channel, not to babysit it

Most Telegram video downloaders are browser gimmicks that choke on large files. GramGrabber is a real MTProto client that treats a 2 GB video like any other file: split it, stream it, verify it.

8 parallel threads, saturated bandwidth

Every video is fetched over 8 concurrent connections, so a download that takes 20 minutes in the Telegram app lands in a fraction of the time. The limit becomes your connection, not the client.

How to download videos from a Telegram channel

With GramGrabber Studio it takes about three minutes, once. After that, grabbing (or just watching) a whole channel is a couple of clicks.

Get your Telegram API credentials

Telegram requires every client app to identify itself. Log in at my.telegram.org with your phone number (the confirmation code arrives inside the Telegram app), open API development tools, and create an application — any name works. You'll receive an api_id and an api_hash.

Treat your api_hash like a password — it's tied to your Telegram account. Never commit it or share it.

Install GramGrabber Studio

Download for macOS (drag to Applications) or Windows, open it and the interface appears in your browser. Paste your api_id and api_hash on the welcome screen — they're stored locally and used only to talk to Telegram.

macOS will warn about an unidentified developer the first time (the app isn't notarized) — right-click the app and choose Open, once.

Sign in, pick a channel, download — or just watch

Sign in to Telegram (phone → code), pick any channel you follow and you'll see every video with size and duration. Select what you want and hit Download — 8 parallel threads, live progress, resume after interruptions — or press ▶ to stream it instantly in the built-in player. Downloads land in your Library, organized by channel.

Prefer the terminal? The CLI in one go

Same engine, no interface. Needs Go 1.25+; credentials go in a local .env:

git clone https://github.com/jordanalmeida/GramGrabber.git
cd GramGrabber
cp env.example .env    # fill in APP_ID and APP_HASH
go build -o gram-grabber .
./gram-grabber         # sign in, pick a channel, done

Frequently asked questions

Is GramGrabber really free?

Yes — free and open source, forever. No ads, no premium tier, no download limits, no accounts, no telemetry. You can read every line of code on GitHub.

Is it safe to use with my Telegram account?

GramGrabber connects directly to Telegram through the official MTProto API — the same protocol the official apps use — with your own credentials. Nothing passes through third-party servers, and your session file stays on your machine.

Can I resume an interrupted download?

Yes. GramGrabber detects partially downloaded files and offers to continue exactly where the transfer stopped — even after a crash, a network drop or a reboot.

Can I watch videos without downloading them?

Yes. In GramGrabber Studio, hit ▶ on any video and it streams straight from Telegram with seeking, speed control and picture-in-picture — nothing is saved to disk unless you choose to download.

Does it work with private channels?

It works with any channel your Telegram account can access, including private channels you're a member of. It cannot (and will not) access channels you don't belong to.

Why does it need my own api_id and api_hash?

Telegram requires every MTProto client to identify itself with API credentials. Using your own keeps the connection strictly between you and Telegram — GramGrabber never sees, stores or transmits them anywhere else. Getting them takes two minutes at my.telegram.org (covered in step 1 of the tutorial).

Is there a graphical version?

Yes — GramGrabber Studio, with installers for macOS and Windows (plus a Linux binary): browse channels visually, download with live progress and watch videos in a built-in player with speed control, picture-in-picture and cinema mode. The classic CLI is also included.

Free software, in the honest sense

No "free trial", no locked features, no email wall. GramGrabber is open source: use it, audit it, fork it. If it saves you an afternoon, a ⭐ on GitHub is the only thank-you it asks for — and issues & pull requests are always welcome.
